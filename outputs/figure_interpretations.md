# Interpretation of EDA figures (generated from the output tables)

**fig01_severity_distribution.png**  
Most reported collisions are Slight (73.8%, n=74,881); 24.8% are Serious (n=25,191) and 1.4% Fatal (n=1,453). Combined, 26.2% of collisions were Fatal or Serious (KSI). The distribution is strongly imbalanced, so Fatal collisions are rare events and any category-level comparison of fatal outcomes rests on small counts.

**fig02_lighting_weather_distribution.png**  
71.7% of collisions with a recorded lighting condition occurred in daylight and 28.3% in darkness; within darkness, lit streets dominate (20.4% of all collisions), followed by unlit roads with no lighting (5.6%), unknown lighting (1.6%) and lights unlit (0.7%). Weather was 'fine, no high winds' in 82.2% of collisions and 'raining, no high winds' in 10.1%; fog/mist and every snow category are each below 0.5%. 'Other' and 'Unknown' account for 5.6%. These are shares of reported collisions, not of journeys, so they do not show that fine weather or daylight are more dangerous.

**fig03_monthly_pattern.png**  
Monthly collision counts are fairly stable: from 7,155 (Feb) to 9,188 (Nov). February is the shortest month; per day, the range is 256 (Feb) to 306 (Nov). In contrast the share of collisions in darkness varies strongly with season, from 12.4% in Jul to 55.1% in Dec, following day length. Lighting condition is therefore tied to the calendar (and, as Figure 4 shows, to the hour), which matters when interpreting any lighting-severity association.

**fig04_hourly_pattern.png**  
On weekdays collisions peak at 08:00 (23.3 per day) and, higher, at 17:00 (27.2 per day), consistent with commuting hours; on weekends there is one broad afternoon peak around 14:00 (17.5 per day) and more collisions per day than on weekdays in every hour from 00:00 to 04:59. The share of collisions that are Fatal/Serious is highest overnight (up to 33.1% at 04:00; at least 31.6% in every hour from 00:00 to 04:59) and lowest at 08:00 (21.4%), against 26.2% overall. Weekend days also show a higher KSI share (Saturday 28.4%, Sunday 28.6%) than Monday-Friday (25.1-25.9%). The busiest hours are not the most severe hours.

**fig05_severity_by_lighting.png**  
Compared with daylight (KSI 25.3%, Fatal 1.2%), the Fatal/Serious share is higher in lit darkness (26.8%, Fatal 1.3%), with unlit lights (28.9%, Fatal 2.6%, n=720) and especially with no lighting (KSI 36.3%, Fatal 4.7%, 3.9 times the daylight fatal share). Darkness with unknown lighting is the exception (KSI 23.4%, Fatal 1.0%), a category likely linked to collisions recorded without police attendance (see Figure 6 note). The pattern is descriptive: rural roads are over-represented in unlit darkness (rural share 90% of 'no lighting' collisions vs 34% in daylight), so lighting is entangled with road environment.

**fig06_ksi_by_road_weather.png**  
(a) The KSI share rises with speed limit from 22.4% (20 mph) through 24.8% (30), 27.3% (40) and 29.6% (50) to 36.4% (60 mph); it is lower at 70 mph (25.5%), so the relationship is not monotonic. (b) Single carriageways (27.9%) exceed dual carriageways (24.6%), one-way streets (20.6%), slip roads (20.0%) and roundabouts (19.5%). (c) Weather shows little difference between the two common categories (fine 26.8%, rain 26.1%); fog/mist and fine-with-high-wind categories are higher (about 32%) but are based on few collisions. The very low 'Unknown' weather share (12.5%) is likely a reporting effect: 94% of 'Unknown weather' collisions had no police officer attending, and KSI share is 31.8% where an officer attended vs 16.3% (no attendance) and 12.1% (self-completion form). (d) Rural collisions are more often Fatal/Serious (30.9%) than urban ones (23.9%).
